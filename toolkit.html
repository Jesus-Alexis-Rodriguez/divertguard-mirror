Diversion Investigation Audit Worksheet (PDF and Word)

When to use it. Work down the questions in order. The first reconciliation comes before the upstream records, and the interview comes after both, so you ask about specific transactions. Write the answer, or the place the record is kept, beside each question, and the date it was answered. The federal notification clock runs from discovery, so write that date and time at question 2 before anything else.

Download PDF Download Word (.docx) 27 questions in 6 sections, each with its source

Read the 27 questions here

1. Start: what is the discrepancy?

  1. Which drug, strength and form is affected, and what is the difference between the expected and the counted quantity? Source: Count records
  2. When was the discrepancy discovered, by whom and how? Write the date and time: the federal notification clock runs from discovery. Source: 21 CFR 1301.76(b)
  3. Which period does the review cover, from the last count that matched to the day of discovery? Source: Count records
  4. Which people, units and systems had access to this drug in that period? Source: Knight et al., AJHP 2022

2. First reconciliation: one person, one window

  1. For each person with access in the window, what does the dispensing cabinet record as dispensed? Export every transaction with its timestamp. Source: Knight et al., AJHP 2022
  2. For each dispense, what does the medication administration record show as given, to which patient and at what time? Source: Knight et al., AJHP 2022
  3. For each dispense, was any remainder wasted, who witnessed it, and does the waste record match the quantity left? Source: Knight et al., AJHP 2022
  4. Which dispenses have no administration entry, no order, or a waste with no witness? Source: Knight et al., AJHP 2022
  5. Who ran this reconciliation, how many hours did it take, and is it someone other than the person whose transactions were reviewed? Source: Knight et al., AJHP 2022

3. Upstream records: where else could the drug have left?

  1. Do wholesaler shipments for this drug match what was received into the inventory system, in full? Source: Knight et al., AJHP 2022
  2. Do movements out of the central pharmacy or vault match what was restocked into the cabinet? Source: Knight et al., AJHP 2022
  3. Did anyone access the drug at a time they were not clocked in? Compare time-clock records with transaction times. Source: Knight et al., AJHP 2022
  4. Do the dispensing patterns line up with the pain scores documented in the patient record? Source: Knight et al., AJHP 2022
  5. Are the receiving logs, perpetual inventory and last physical count complete and unaltered? Records must be kept at least two years. Source: 21 CFR 1304.04

4. The interview

  1. Which specific transactions will you ask about, and is the reconciliation in hand before the interview? Source: Knight et al., AJHP 2022
  2. Who conducts the interview: a trained drug diversion professional, with a leadership team of department heads reviewing the findings? Source: Knight et al., AJHP 2022
  3. What did the person, or their supervisor, say about each transaction? Record it in their words. Source: Knight et al., AJHP 2022

5. Filings and notifications

  1. Is this a theft or significant loss? Record the factors weighed, including the quantity lost in relation to the type of business and the specific substance, and the reason for the decision either way. Source: 21 CFR 1301.76(b)
  2. Was written notice sent to the DEA Field Division Office within one business day of discovery? Record the date and time sent. Source: 21 CFR 1301.76(b)
  3. Was a complete DEA Form 106 filed within 45 days after discovery? Record the filing date. Source: 21 CFR 1301.76(b)
  4. Which state notification applies, when does its clock start, and was it met? See the state law pages on this site. Source: State law pages
  5. Did the investigation begin with an employee report? If so, is that employee's identity protected, and have all employees been told the reporting policy? Source: 21 CFR 1301.91

6. What a regulator will ask afterward

  1. Is the current inventory accurate, and is the biennial inventory complete and on file? Source: 21 CFR 1304.11; DOJ E.D. Cal. 2026
  2. Are the controlled substances kept in a secure location, and who has access to it? Source: DOJ D.N.J. release
  3. Is supervision of employees who have access adequate, and is it documented? Source: DOJ D.N.J. release
  4. Are the controlled substance order records complete? Source: DOJ E.D. Cal. 2026
  5. Which corrective actions have been decided, who owns each, and by what date? Source: Program record

Sources: 21 CFR 1301.76, 1301.91, 1304.04 and 1304.11 (eCFR); Knight et al., Am J Health Syst Pharm 2022 (PMC9353695); U.S. Attorney's Office press releases for the District of New Jersey and the Eastern District of California. Educational content, not legal advice.

ADC override review worksheet

When to use it. Use it when a pharmacist or diversion analyst reviews automated dispensing cabinet (ADC) overrides: removals made without a verified order. One row per override reviewed.

What to fill in.

  1. Export the override report from your cabinet system for the period and paste one row per override into the first seven columns.
  2. Check each override against the order record and the medication administration record, then complete order_match, witness (if the removal involved one) and the reviewer column.
  3. Record the disposition in the words your policy uses, and the date it was decided. A blank disposition date means the row is still open.

Discrepancy and cycle-count log

When to use it. Use it to record every count that does not match, whether it came from a routine cycle count or a cabinet discrepancy alert, and to follow each one to a root cause and a closing date. One row per discrepancy.

What to fill in.

  1. Enter the unit, date, item, the expected count and the counted quantity when the mismatch is found.
  2. Work out variance as counted minus expected, so a shortage is negative. Use the same sign convention on every row.
  3. Name the investigator, then fill in root cause and resolution as they are established. Fill in closed_date only when the resolution is documented.

Waste documentation log

When to use it. Use it for a manual record of controlled-substance waste, or for an audit sample pulled from the system so each waste event can be traced to its witness and to the cabinet or pharmacy record. One row per waste event.

What to fill in.

  1. Enter the date, the drug and strength, the amount drawn up and the amount wasted, each with its unit.
  2. Record the witness, where the waste went and the method used, in the terms your waste policy uses.
  3. In reconciliation_reference put the cabinet transaction ID or pharmacy record number that ties this waste to a removal, so the two can be matched.

Investigation case file template and case index

When to use it. Open one case file for every investigation, from the day it is opened to the day it is closed. It records the facts, who was told and when, what was preserved, which outside bodies were considered and how the outcome is supported. The case index is the one-line-per-case register that sits above the files.

What to fill in.

  1. Make a copy of the template for each case and give it a case ID that is not built from a name.
  2. Work down the sections in order. Write “not applicable” and why, rather than leaving a step blank.
  3. Add a row to the case index when the case opens and update its dates as they happen.

Before you adopt these

These are blank working files, not a finished program. Adopt them safely:

  • Treat the filled files as confidential. A completed override worksheet or case index points at named staff. Keep copies in an access-controlled location, not a shared drive or a personal email account, and decide with your compliance officer who may open them.
  • Keep patient identifiers out unless you need them. Use a transaction or record reference that lets an authorised person find the patient in the source system, rather than copying patient names or record numbers into a spreadsheet.
  • A row is not a finding. An override with no matching order, or a variance with no explanation yet, is a question to answer. Record the explanation or the escalation; do not record a conclusion about a person.
  • Separate the people. The reviewer should not be the user whose override is being reviewed, or the witness on that row. Say so in your policy.
  • Your system of record still governs. These files support review and documentation; they do not replace the controlled-substance records your cabinet, pharmacy and electronic health record keep. See the recordkeeping checklist.
  • Have policy, HR and counsel review before use. Replace the example categories (dispositions, outcome categories, notification list) with your own, and have your compliance officer, human resources and counsel confirm them. Nothing here is legal advice, and which outside bodies must be told depends on your facility and state.
  • Control versions. Keep one master copy of each blank, date your changes, and file completed copies under your retention policy.
