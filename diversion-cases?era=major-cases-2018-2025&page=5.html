Minnesota Pharmacist in Charge Pleads Guilty to Stealing Oxycodone Bottles From Inventory

Maplewood, Minnesota

The pharmacist in charge at a pharmacy in Maplewood, Minnesota, took bottles of oxycodone from the pharmacy's inventory for personal use from March 2020 to June 2023 and falsified the pharmacy's records to conceal it. The pharmacist pleaded guilty on April 25, 2024, to one count of obtaining a controlled substance by misrepresentation. Sentencing was to be scheduled at a later time.

2024-04-25

Norman Doctor Pays $60,000 in Records Settlement

Norman and Ardmore, Oklahoma

In April 2024, Dr. Ronnie Keith, who owns and operates Keith Medical Clinics in Norman and Ardmore, Oklahoma, paid $60,000 to settle civil penalty claims under the Comprehensive Drug Abuse Prevention and Control Act. The United States alleged that from August 10, 2021, through August 10, 2023, he failed to maintain invoices for certain controlled substance purchases and failed to document dates of receipt. It also alleged he did not keep an initial inventory or take a new inventory biennially. Keith did not admit liability.

2024-04-25

Missouri Anesthesiologist Admits Taking Fentanyl

Kansas City, Missouri

In April 2024, anesthesiologist Roman Peplinski, 37, of Kansas City, Missouri, pleaded guilty in federal court to one count of obtaining a controlled substance by misrepresentation, fraud, forgery, deception, or subterfuge. He admitted illegally obtaining fentanyl from Lakewood Medical Center in Lee's Summit on November 5, 2022, falsely saying it was for a patient who had already been discharged. He also admitted withdrawing fentanyl from three vials between September and November 2022. He faces up to four years in prison, and sentencing will be set later.

2024-04-22

New Haven Pharmacy and Owner Settle CSA for $120,000

New Haven, Connecticut

In April 2024, Cornerstone Pharmacy, Inc., doing business as Whalley Drug in New Haven, Connecticut, and its owner, pharmacist Yong Kwon, paid $120,000 to resolve Controlled Substances Act allegations. The government alleged that between September 2017 and June 2021 they filled controlled substance prescriptions issued without a legitimate medical purpose, and that a DEA audit found recordkeeping discrepancies. On June 22, 2021, Kwon agreed to voluntarily surrender the pharmacy's DEA license, and it no longer operates as a retail pharmacy.

2024-04-18

West Virginia Doctor Convicted Over Adderall Scripts

Bridgeport, West Virginia

In April 2024, a federal jury in Clarksburg found David Elwood Hess, 60, of Bridgeport, West Virginia, guilty of four counts of improper prescribing and one count of destroying records in a federal investigation. The jury found that Hess wrote Adderall prescriptions to his patients without a legitimate medical purpose and beyond the bounds of professional practice. It also found that he remotely wiped his iPhone after law enforcement seized it. The Drug Enforcement Administration investigated, and Hess will be sentenced after a pre-sentence investigation report is completed.

2024-04-10

New Jersey drug manufacturer settles claims it failed to account for oxycodone, hydrocodone and temazepam

A pharmaceutical manufacturer, a subsidiary of a larger drug company, agreed to pay $2.25 million to resolve allegations that it violated the Controlled Substances Act by failing to account for approximately 3.1 kg of oxycodone, 7.7 kg of hydrocodone and 30 kg of temazepam between January 2019 and August 2021. DEA identified the alleged violations during on-site inspections that began in the summer of 2021. The company also entered a memorandum of agreement with DEA requiring operational improvements for three years. The claims settled are allegations only, and there has been no determination of liability.

Ardmore, OK

The nurse was sentenced to 12 months in prison for one count of Tampering with Consumer Products.

2024-03-06

Florida Doctor Sentenced Over Post-Suspension Scripts

Tampa, Florida

Tommy Louisville, 71, of Pembroke Pines, Florida, was sentenced in Tampa federal court on March 6, 2024, to one year and one day in prison for unlawful drug distribution, false statements relating to health care matters, and Paycheck Protection Program fraud. He was a Florida-licensed physician and DEA registrant whose license the Florida Board of Medicine suspended on May 30, 2019. Court documents state he continued to issue controlled-substance prescriptions after the suspension and concealed that from Medicare when submitting the prescriptions for payment. He pleaded guilty on November 3, 2023, and was ordered to pay restitution to the Small Business Administration and Medicare.

2024-03

Former Scripps Encinitas anesthesiologist placed on seven-year probation for taking and self-administering propofol and opioids

The Medical Board of California placed a former Scripps Memorial Hospital Encinitas anesthesiologist on seven years' probation through a stipulated settlement and disciplinary order, with the license revocation stayed. According to the order, the physician told board investigators she had self-administered propofol during a May 2022 shift and had taken home vials of leftover fentanyl, hydromorphone and midazolam roughly a dozen times the prior month. Probation conditions include drug testing, practice monitoring, psychotherapy, a substance abuse support group, patient notification and a bar on solo practice. The physician has not worked at a Scripps facility since May 2022.

2024-03

Eastern Kentucky physician and clinic nurse sentenced for conspiracy to issue illegitimate opioid prescriptions

A physician who practiced at several Pikeville-area clinics and a nurse who worked at the same clinics were convicted at trial of conspiring to unlawfully distribute controlled substances through prescriptions written outside the usual course of professional practice. Evidence showed large monthly quantities of opioids, sometimes combined with benzodiazepines, and that the physician wrote multiple oxycodone prescriptions for the nurse, who also signed the physician's name to prescriptions. In early 2024 a federal judge sentenced the physician to 100 months and the nurse to 60 months in prison.

2024-02-29

Celina Physician Indicted in Drug Distribution Case

Sherman, Texas (Eastern District of Texas)

Kevin Shelton, 53, a physician from Celina, Texas, and eight others were indicted by a federal grand jury in the Eastern District of Texas in February 2024. Shelton was charged with conspiracy to possess with intent to manufacture and distribute controlled substances and conspiracy to commit money laundering. The defendants are alleged to have conspired to distribute substances including hydrocodone, alprazolam, tramadol, Adderall and dextroamphetamine. They made initial appearances on February 29, 2024. If convicted, each faces up to 20 years in federal prison. An indictment is not evidence of guilt, and all defendants are presumed innocent.

Texas VA Pharmacy Tech Sentenced to 42 Months

San Antonio, Texas (Western District of Texas)

Scott Mitchell Brown, 38, of Kerrville, Texas, was sentenced in San Antonio federal court to 42 months in prison for conspiracy to possess with intent to distribute a controlled substance, theft of government property and possession of stolen mail. He was a pharmacy technician at the Veterans Affairs Medical Center in Kerrville. Court documents state he devised a scheme to steal narcotics being shipped from the pharmacy to VA patients and sold them in the Kerr County area. With a codefendant he also stole prescribed narcotics from veterans' mailboxes. Two codefendants were sentenced earlier, to 12 months and 35 months.

Georgia Doctor Indicted in Alleged Pill-Mill Scheme

Roswell and Buford, Georgia

Isaac Sved, 65, an Atlanta-area physician who operated clinics FamCare in Roswell and Valere Medical PC in Buford, Georgia, was arraigned in February 2024 on federal charges including conspiracy to possess with intent to distribute controlled substances, after a grand jury indicted him in January 2024. The indictment alleged he prescribed excessive amounts of controlled substances, including oxycodone, alprazolam and carisoprodol, with minimal or no examinations, and falsified patient files. Three others were also indicted. The charges are allegations, and the defendants are presumed innocent.

2024-01-31

Baldwin Pharmacy Consent Decree Over Opioid Dispensing

Baldwin, Michigan

In January 2024, a federal judge in the Western District of Michigan entered a consent decree against Baldwin Pattie Drug Store, LLC, a pharmacy in Baldwin, Michigan, and its owner, Matthew Krawczak. The United States alleged they dispensed hydrocodone to patients they believed were abusing drugs and failed to keep accurate dispensing records, with an audit finding thousands of unaccounted-for opioids. The decree permanently barred the pharmacy from dispensing controlled substances, required surrender of its DEA registration, barred Krawczak from dispensing for ten years unless supervised, and set a civil penalty of $10,000 to $30,000. Liability was not determined.

According to court documents and facts admitted in open court, the technician used the position at a Jefferson County pharmacy to acquire large numbers of prescription hydrocodone pills from the pharmacy's inventory. Sentencing was set for April 30, 2024, with a statutory maximum of four years in prison.

San Antonio Doctor Gets 30 Months for Opioid Scripts

San Antonio, Texas

Paul Silva, 48, a former San Antonio, Texas physician, was sentenced in January 2024 to 30 months in federal prison for conspiring to dispense controlled substances without a legitimate medical purpose. He admitted issuing more than 500 prescriptions for more than 40,000 dosage units, primarily opioids, in the names of living and deceased family members, former patients and others after his license expired and was then suspended. Co-conspirators picked up the medications and distributed them. Co-defendants Denton Thomas and Nicholas Klumpp pleaded guilty; Klumpp was sentenced to 27 months in September 2023.

Weston Doctor Convicted of Unlawful Opioid Dispensing

Miami, Florida (Weston resident)

Osmin Morales, 72, of Weston, Florida, a doctor who ran a purported pain management clinic, was convicted by a Miami federal jury on January 12, 2024, of conspiracy to unlawfully dispense and distribute controlled substances and six counts of unlawfully dispensing them. Evidence showed he issued prescriptions for oxycodone, morphine and alprazolam without appropriate medical basis, often without examining patients or while absent from the clinic, and pre-wrote prescriptions handed out by office managers for $250 cash payments. Sentencing was scheduled for April 17, and he faced up to 20 years per count.

2024-01-17

Valley Center Veterinary Clinic Pays $125,000 Over Controlled-Substance Mishandling

Valley Center, California

Best Veterinary Care, doing business as Valley Center Veterinary Clinic in San Diego County, California, paid $125,000 to resolve allegations that it violated the Controlled Substances Act between 2020 and 2022. A DEA inventory audit found the clinic could not account for pills of the benzodiazepines alprazolam and diazepam and the opioid tramadol, and that it lacked effective controls to guard against theft and diversion of its controlled-substance stock. The clinic entered a Memorandum of Agreement with the DEA requiring additional safeguards for handling controlled substances going forward.

West Bloomfield Doctor Gets 5 Years for Prescribing

West Bloomfield, Michigan

Scott Henry Cooper, 61, a primary care physician at Comprehensive Medical Associates in West Bloomfield, Michigan, was sentenced in January 2024 to five years in federal prison after pleading guilty to unlawful prescribing of controlled substances. He admitted that during 2013 to 2018 he continued prescribing oxycodone and other drugs to patients he did not routinely see. For one patient, he wrote monthly prescriptions totaling over 7,000 dosage units for almost three years while the patient was in prison, and a relative picked them up. He has not been allowed to prescribe controlled substances since 2020.
