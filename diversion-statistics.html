Drugs, settings, outcomes, and year-over-year counts from the DivertGuard public case registry.

Where Does Diversion Happen?

Retail pharmacy settings appear in 335 of 1087 cases (31%). Hospital / health system follows at 254 of 1087 (23%).

Lower-volume settings still appear too: long-term care (45), home health / hospice (30), ambulatory surgery / or (29), va / federal facility (17), dental / oral surgery (17), emergency services / ems (16), fertility / reproductive (2).

Setting counts sum higher than the 613 cases with an identifiable setting because several cases span more than one environment — for example a health system that also operated dispensing pharmacies. How Did the Cases Resolve?

355 of 1087 cases have reached sentencing — the furthest stage most records document — and 95 are still only charged or indicted. A case can pass through several stages, so raw mentions overlap (465 records mention a sentence, 405 a guilty plea); the chart counts each case once, in its furthest stage, which is why the bars add up to 1087.

The practical takeaway for compliance teams: a large share of these matters are still in progress at the time of documentation. Charges are allegations until a conviction is recorded.

Cases Documented by Year (2018–2026)

Case volume in the registry is concentrated in the most recent years: 144 cases in 2024, against 34 in 2018 (2026 is a partial year). The registry also catalogues cases from 1983-2026, which are shown in the case list and map but are too thin to chart on this scale.

Read this as documentation activity rather than incidence. Recent enforcement actions are easier to find and are added to the registry sooner, so the curve reflects both prosecutions and the pace of collection.

Cases by year, one chart per state

Each chart counts the registry's cases for one state by the year of the charge, plea or settlement announcement, for 2018–2026 plus a grey bar for everything dated earlier. All 52 states and territories with at least one catalogued case are shown, most cases first, which is 1087 of the 1087 cases; 0 jurisdictions have none yet. A short bar means few records have been catalogued for that state, not that diversion is rare there, and 2026 is a partial year. Every chart is drawn from the same case list as the map above, so its bars sum to the count under its name.

Under each chart, the source line says how the state's cases are sourced. Each case record stores one source link, which its case page shows, and the first line counts those records by the source type recorded for them: primary (a government or court document, such as a U.S. Attorney's Office or inspector general release), news (press report), or unclassified (no type recorded yet). Across the registry that is 737 primary, 179 news and 171 unclassified of 1087. The second line names the most frequent publishing sites. Each state name links to its case page where one exists, and the state law pages cover statute and board-rule citations for California, Texas, Florida, New York, Pennsylvania, Ohio, Michigan, New Jersey, Illinois and Georgia.

Key Findings

  • Oxycodone is named in 324 of 1087 cases (30%) — ahead of hydrocodone (198) and fentanyl (180).
  • Retail pharmacy settings appear in 335 of 1087 cases (31%), with hospital / health system second at 254 of 1087 (23%).
  • 144 cases are dated 2024 — the largest single year in the registry, and 9 years in the 1983-2026 range (1985, 1986, 1987, 1988, 1989, 1992, 1996, 1998, 2002) carry no catalogued case.
  • 355 cases have reached sentencing — the furthest stage the record documents — and 95 are still only charged or indicted. A further 465 records mention a sentence and 405 a guilty plea; those overlap with the stages above, so they are not additional cases.
  • Catalogued cases span 52 states and territories (1087 of 1087 mapped) — Maryland has the most with 52.
  • Physician / surgeon is the most common role, in 295 of the 970 cases that name a clinician (30%) — ahead of pharmacist at 155. 117 cases name no role at all.
  • Only 89 of 1087 records state a countable quantity taken — 998 give no total, which is itself a finding about how records are written.
  • A specific drug is named in 709 of 1087 case records and a practice setting is identifiable in 613 of 1087 — public case documents do not always specify either.

Methodology

The registry is compiled from public federal and state court records and news reporting. Drugs and practice settings are classified from each individual case record: not every case names a drug, and not every case identifies a setting, so the denominators differ (a drug is named in 709 of 1087 cases, a setting is identifiable in 613 of 1087). A single case may involve more than one substance, which is why drug counts sum higher than 709. Percentages are rounded to the nearest whole number.

This is a documentation snapshot, not a statistical sample. It should not be used to estimate the prevalence of diversion nationally. Use it to understand what documented cases look like — which substances and settings recur, and how those matters proceed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most commonly diverted drug in the registry?

In this registry, oxycodone. It is named in 324 of 1087 documented cases (30%), followed by hydrocodone in 198 cases and fentanyl in 180.

Which healthcare setting sees the most diversion cases?

Retail pharmacy, with 335 of 1087 cases (31%). Hospital / health system is second at 254 of 1087 (23%). The remaining settings together account for 156 cases.

Are drug diversion cases increasing?

Documented cases per year are concentrated in recent years: 144 in 2024 — the largest year recorded. That trend describes documentation and enforcement activity captured in public records, not a measured change in how often diversion occurs.

How do drug diversion cases usually end?

Of the 1087 cases, 355 have reached sentencing, 95 are documented at the indicted or charged stage, and 405 records note a guilty plea. These stages overlap, since a case can be charged, plead out, and be sentenced over time.

How often do public case records name a specific drug or setting?

A specific drug is named in 709 of the 1087 cases, and a practice setting is identifiable in 613 of 1087. The remainder describe controlled substances or employers only in general terms, which is why the percentages on this page use 1087 cases as the denominator.
